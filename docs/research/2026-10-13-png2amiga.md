# png2amiga: what Convert64 can learn (2026-10-13)

**Source:** <https://github.com/tinic/png2amiga> at `4a87776` (v1.111.2, 2026-10-05), MIT. It has the same author as png2c64 and a web app at png2amiga.app. It was read as data; nothing was run.

> **Update 2026-10-18:** ADR-013 (moving the reference to png2amiga) was tried in PR #273 and **reversed by ADR-014**. Jaakko compared the two by eye on four sheets: png2c64's baseline won on 5 of 6 fixtures, Match range and Refine made things worse, and S2 disagreed with the eye on every sheet (see the review comments on #273). png2c64 stays the reference. png2amiga stays as a comparison solver (`--solver png2amiga`); its ideas are adopted one at a time, each judged by eye. The "decision needed" and the recommendations below are kept for history.

## The headline: png2amiga is png2c64's successor

- **png2c64 has stopped moving.** Its last commit, 2026-04-15, is exactly the `1265329a` we pinned for ADR-010.
- **png2amiga is very active.** It started in July 2026 and has had several releases a week.
- **Its C64 encoder began as a merge of png2c64.** `src/c64.hpp` calls it "the first proof-of-fit of the png2c64 → png2amiga merge".
- **It now covers more C64 than png2c64 did:** multicolor, hi-res, FLI, AFLI, PETSCII, and custom charsets (hi-res and multicolor) with a tile budget. It also has its own VICE pixel checker (`tools/check_c64_vice.py`).
- **The ported reference is now frozen.** ADR-010 made a port of png2c64 our reference solver, so our reference no longer improves while its author keeps improving the same algorithms in png2amiga. **Decision needed:** keep png2c64 as the frozen bar, or move the reference to png2amiga's C64 path (a new ADR).

## Learnings, by value

### High

1. **Downscaling: Lanczos-3 widened by the shrink ratio** (`src/scale.cpp`). The author's own comment says:
   - the earlier under-filtering made images "look sharper" but actually aliased;
   - sharpness should come from the kernel, "not from leaking high frequencies".

   This is the fix for #235, from the same author. png2c64's 4-tap Mitchell, which we copied in ADR-012, is the older behaviour.
2. **SSIMULACRA2 as the quality score.** Every result is scored with SSIMULACRA2 ("S2", Cloudinary 2022), shown next to PSNR in the CLI and web UI (`src/pipeline.hpp`, `ssimulacra2.hpp`, `tools/bench_ssimulacra2.cpp`). Its scale runs to 100: 30 low, 50 fair, 70 high, 90 visually lossless. For us:
   - It is the metric our sheets lacked. Per-pixel and blurred MSE both misled us in #156.
   - It is a better number for the app's status bar than the clash count (usability review M1).
3. **`--cell-refine`: joint colour and pattern refinement across cell boundaries** (`src/cell_refine.hpp`, `src/cell_graph_refine.hpp`; applies to all C64 modes).
   - It is coordinate descent on a global 3 × 3 binomial blur objective that includes each cell's one-pixel halo, in linear RGB and OKLab, for up to four passes.
   - It keeps the result only if the full-image S2 improves.
   - For ordered dithers it changes colour registers but keeps the pattern phase.

   This addresses png2c64's main weakness, that cells are solved independently. It would be an opt-in "Refine" step after the solve.
4. **Charset modes with a tile budget and reserve** (`--tile-budget N`, `--tile-reserve N`, Hamming-distance merging of glyph pairs when content overflows). This is the *No Glory* map idea (`docs/ideas/map-targets.md`): the level gets 256 − N glyphs, with N reserved for the HUD. The same author has already built the merge step.

### Medium

5. **More dither methods** (`src/dither.hpp`). These are new to our catalogue (`docs/reference/dither-methods.md`):

   | Method | How it works | Value for C64 |
   |---|---|---|
   | `opt-checker`, `opt-line`, `opt-line-checker`, `opt-vline`, `opt-vline-checker` | palette-aware: picks the best 2- or 4-colour plan for a 2 × 2 phase | high — exactly the C64 patterns sceners draw by hand |
   | `tri-tone` | Yliluoma: 2 × 2, three colours at 50/25/25 % | high on a 16-colour palette |
   | `floyd-steinberg-oklab` | FS with the residual carried in OKLab | medium; the author measured +0.3–1.0 S2 on photos and worse results on logos |
   | `structure-fs`, `contrast-fs` | FS with a Laplacian- or contrast-modulated threshold | medium: keeps edges |
   | `gilbert` | error diffusion along a Gilbert curve | medium |
   | `dbs` | Direct Binary Search: greedy descent on an HVS-blurred OKLab cost, warm-started from FS; 5–30 s | high as a "final quality" option |
   | `void-cluster`, `cluster-noise`, `fractal16`, `ign-triangle`, `r2-triangle`, `cranley-bayer` | better noise and ordered thresholds; triangle remaps reduce banding | medium |
   | `bayer3x3`, `bayer5x5`, `bayer6x6`, `bayer7x7`, `v4x2`, `bayer4x2`, `bayer2x4` | non-power-of-2 and non-square matrices | low–medium |
   | `aseprite-old`, `libcaca-3x3`, `libcaca-6x6`, `pegasus-8x8`, `quasicrystal`, `truchet` | hand-tuned and stylistic patterns | low (Effects) |

6. **Dither thumbnails** (`web/scripts/gen-dither-thumbs.mjs`, `web/src/components/DitherGallery.vue`).
   - They are generated at build time: a 32 × 32, 45° black-to-white ramp in 2 colours.
   - The ramp carries **±2/255 seeded jitter**, because without it error-diffusion kernels lock into regular patterns that look like ordered dithers.
   - The gallery is grouped by family; there is no search and no hover preview.
   - Our picker (UX.md §6a) goes further, with live thumbnails in the current palette and hover preview, and adopts the jitter.
7. **The web UI.**
   - Sample images are one click away under the drop zone (usability review M10).
   - The dither dropdown has ▲▼ step buttons beside it.
   - A plain stats line under the result: size, colours, disk size, error.
   - Export is a row of format buttons (png / iff / adf).
8. **C64 metric default.** The README lists `--c64-metric blur` (Pappas–Neuhoff) as the default; the header comment in `c64.hpp` still says mse. *Inferred:* the default changed to blur and the comment is stale.

### Low / later

9. **A palette quantiser family** (median-cut, PNN, OCS brute force, `--best` multi-restart) for Amiga-style free palettes. It is not needed for the fixed C64 palette, but the multi-restart idea carries over to background choice and refinement.
10. **Alpha dithering, `--trim`, `--crop-auto`, `--transparent-color`.** These are useful for sprite sheets (E11).
11. **Performance:** an OKLab SIMD path (`oklab_simd.hpp`), a Metal quantiser (`quantize_metal.cpp`), and `PrecomputedImage` to skip repeated sRGB encoding in hot loops.

## Recommendations

1. **#235** — switch the large-shrink fix from "mip chain + Mitchell" to png2amiga's widened Lanczos-3, or at least use it as the comparison row on the sheet. Keep png2c64 exact for ≤ 4× until the reference decision below is made.
2. **New ADR (decision for Jaakko): which tool is the reference?**
   - **(a)** Keep png2c64 as the frozen parity bar, and treat png2amiga as a source of ideas.
   - **(b)** Move the reference to png2amiga's C64 path at a pinned commit. Re-port the changed parts (scaler, metric default, refine), and regenerate the baselines with png2amiga.

   (b) keeps Convert64 on the author's living line of work, but it is a re-baselining and costs a week.
3. **Adopt SSIMULACRA2** as the score on every sheet and in `--json`, and as the status bar's quality number. Add it as a TAD §4 dependency, or port it; it is small.
4. **Add the "Optimal" family, Tri-tone and DBS** to the method catalogue, ahead of the stylistic ones.
5. **Plan `--cell-refine`** as an opt-in Refine step after the solve, gated on S2.
6. **For *No Glory* charsets (E10),** study png2amiga's charset encoder and its tile budget and reserve before designing ours.
