# Dithering for very small fixed palettes: theory and tooling survey (for a C64 converter)

Scope: algorithmic/academic and pixel-art-tooling side. Target context: 16 fixed colours, max 4 colours per 4x8 multicolour cell (160x200 pixels, 2:1 pixel aspect). Statements marked **[inference]** are mine, not from a cited source. Statements marked **[unverified]** could not be confirmed from a fetched page.

## 1. Palette-aware ordered (positional) dithering

### 1.1 Yliluoma algorithm 1 (pair mixing)
- Mechanism: for each pixel, search every pair of palette colours and every mixing ratio for the mix nearest the input; the threshold matrix then picks between the two colours. Tri-/quad-tone variants exist but quad-tone is O(N^4) and "too slow". Source: https://bisqwit.iki.fi/story/howto/dither/jy/
- Cost: M x N x (N-1)/2 + N iterations, i.e. O(N^2 x M) per pixel (same page). With N=16, M=64 that is about 7,700 distance tests per pixel, trivial offline.
- Quality: first version used plain Euclidean RGB; the author improved it with luma-weighted (CCIR 601) comparison (same page). No gamma in the algorithm-1 code; a separate section contrasts gamma-unaware and gamma-aware mixing (same page).
- Relevance: pair-only mixing matches the C64 reality that a cell has few colours; easy to constrain the pair to colours allowed in the current cell. **[inference]**

### 1.2 Yliluoma algorithm 2 (greedy candidate list)
- Mechanism: greedily builds a list of up to 16 palette colours (added in power-of-two batches) whose average approximates the input; the list is sorted by luminance and the threshold value indexes into it. Gamma 2.2 is applied to palette values. Cost N x (log2 M + 1) minimum to N x M x log2 M maximum. Default comparison is luma-weighted RGB; CIEDE2000 is included but disabled, and plain CIE76 gave scattered yellow pixels. Source: https://bisqwit.iki.fi/story/howto/dither/jy/
- Strength: gamma-aware, cheap, still purely positional. Artefact: with a 4-colour cell budget the candidate list is dominated by repeated colours, so effectively it degenerates to a 2-3 colour mix. **[inference]**

### 1.3 Yliluoma algorithm 3 (iterative splitting)
- Mechanism: start with the closest single palette colour, then repeatedly split one colour's share into two palette colours whenever that lowers error; final list is luminance-sorted, gamma 2.2. A later variant precomputes 7258 combinations of up to 8 colours filtered by luminance spread to avoid odd-looking mixes. The author calls it "slow, but very thorough". Source: https://bisqwit.iki.fi/story/howto/dither/jy/
- Relevance: the luminance-spread filter is the same idea a C64 converter needs (avoid pairing very different luma, which also reduces flicker, see section 6). **[inference]**

### 1.4 Knoll pattern dithering and how Yliluoma relates
- Yliluoma's page labels Knoll's method "the patented pattern dithering algorithm"; its "algorithm 4" is the Knoll-style method using an error accumulator and per-pixel candidate list, with an 8x8 matrix where the patented one uses 4x4, and the author reuses version-1 ColorCompare "to further avoid infringing on the patent". The page does not state the assignee or expiry date. Source: https://bisqwit.iki.fi/story/howto/dither/jy/ . Adobe assignment and patent expiry: **[unverified]** from fetched material; check before relying on it.
- Key property shared by all Yliluoma variants: a pixel's colour depends only on that pixel (plus position), "making it suitable for animations" (same page).
- Wikipedia on ordered dithering: skipping threshold normalisation "produces incorrect results especially when using a small or arbitrary palette"; recommended map size is at least the source:target colour ratio; classic Bayer shows crosshatch. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordered_dithering

## 2. Error-diffusion families

Standard kernels (Floyd-Steinberg 7/3/5/1 over 16): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floyd%E2%80%93Steinberg_dithering . FS coefficients were found by trial and error aiming for a checkerboard at 50% density; the page says to linearise values first and to avoid wraparound overflow (same page).

| Kernel | Spread | Notes (source) |
|---|---|---|
| Floyd-Steinberg | 4 px, /16 | popular, cheap; "unorganic" flat areas per https://surma.dev/things/ditherpunk/ |
| Jarvis-Judice-Ninke | 12 px, /48 | coarser, fewer visible patterns, slower (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dither ; https://surma.dev/things/ditherpunk/) |
| Stucki | 12 px, /42 | minimal difference from JJN for most images (https://tannerhelland.com/2012/12/28/dithering-eleven-algorithms-source-code.html) |
| Burkes | 7 px, /32 | Stucki without bottom row, power-of-two divisor, "minor hit to quality" (Helland) |
| Sierra / Two-row Sierra / Sierra Lite | /32, /16, /4 | Lite is faster than FS with similar results (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dither ; Helland for coefficients) |
| Atkinson | 6 px, /8 (only 6/8 of error) | higher contrast, less speckle, but dark/bright areas wash out (Helland; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dither says "blown out") |

- Shiau-Fan, Stevenson-Arce: could not locate a fetchable primary source. Ostromoukhov's paper calls Shiau-Fan's method the best-looking in his view but patented (https://scis.uohyd.ac.in/~chakcs/CIP2018/SIGGRAPH01_varcoeffED.pdf). Stevenson-Arce: **[unverified]**. Treat both as low priority.
- Ostromoukhov (SIGGRAPH 2001): 256 coefficient sets (one per input level, found offline by minimising deviation from a blue-noise spectrum), 3 neighbours, serpentine scan. Claims FS produces worms in highlights/shadows and regular patches near 1/3 and 1/4, and that the new method is "nearly artifact-free"; evidence is visual plus spectral, no formal metrics. Colour is described as per-channel extension; no colour results. Source: same PDF as above. The coefficient tables are grey-level-indexed, so they do not map naturally onto an arbitrary-palette residual. **[inference]**
- Zhou-Fang (2003): threshold modulation applied to variable-coefficient error diffusion to remove its remaining artefacts, with parameters found by a cost-function search; authors claim artefact-free over the whole tonal range, supported by Fourier analysis. Source: https://history.siggraph.org/?p=115885 (abstract only fetched).
- Structure-aware error diffusion (Chang, Alain, Ostromoukhov, SIGGRAPH Asia 2009): exists, aimed at structure preservation (listing: https://history.siggraph.org/?p=196623 ; paper PDF https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/ostrom/publications/pdf/SIGGRAPH-ASIA09_saed.pdf). I could not fetch the content (bot wall on HAL), so the mechanism is **[unverified]** here.
- Riemersma: walks pixels along a Hilbert curve, carrying a 1-D queue of recent errors with exponentially decaying weights (author suggests >=16 entries, 1:16 oldest:newest weight). Works with non-uniform palettes; separate error lists per RGB channel. Localised (each pixel affects ~16 neighbours), which reduces frame-to-frame change. Coarser than error diffusion; unweighted queues cause bias. Source: https://www.compuphase.com/riemer.htm . Surma found r=1/8, n=32 looked better and calls the result "very organic, nearly as good as blue noise" but sequential (https://surma.dev/things/ditherpunk/).
- Artefacts, general: error diffusion produces edge enhancement (error pushes next pixel dark after light-to-dark transition etc.), 1-D diffusion gives vertical lines, serpentine scanning reduces artefacts (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error_diffusion). "Worms" are named in the Ostromoukhov paper (above). Helland: serpentine "can reduce uniform speckling"; results also depend on threshold, luminance formula, gamma and int vs float error tracking (https://tannerhelland.com/2012/12/28/dithering-eleven-algorithms-source-code.html).
- Error clamping: the FS page warns that integer wrap-around can invert black/white and that clipping is best avoided in that context (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floyd%E2%80%93Steinberg_dithering). For a 16-colour palette with large gamut gaps, unbounded accumulated error can "run away" across a flat region; clamping or attenuating the diffused error (cf. Atkinson's 6/8) is the usual remedy. The runaway claim is **[inference]**; Atkinson's partial diffusion is sourced above.
- What works for low colour counts (evidence): sources agree error diffusion handles arbitrary palettes while plain ordered dithering needs evenly spaced levels (https://surma.dev/things/ditherpunk/ ; Wikipedia ordered dithering above). Yliluoma's page is the main source that positional dithering can match arbitrary palettes. No fetched source gave a controlled head-to-head on 16-colour sets; the ranking in section 7 is therefore partly judgment.

## 3. Blue noise, interleaved gradient noise, R2

- Blue noise: when patterning is visible it is the "least unsightly and distracting"; error diffusion was one of the first blue-noise producers, and ordered dithering with a blue-noise matrix (void-and-cluster) "looks closer to error diffusion". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dither
- Void-and-cluster (Ulichney 1993): Gaussian energy (sigma 1.5 in paper, author prefers 1.9) to find voids and clusters, three phases, ranks scaled to bit depth, toroidal wrap so the texture tiles seamlessly. Slow offline (about 24 s at 128x128 and 400 s at 256x256 in the author's CPU implementation; faster implementations reported by commenters). Source: https://blog.demofox.org/2019/06/25/generating-blue-noise-textures-with-void-and-cluster/ . The same page says the original algorithm was patented and expired (author's statement; not independently checked).
- Tile sizes: Surma generated 64x64 (https://surma.dev/things/ditherpunk/); Wronski uses a 64x64 wrapping texture and says it avoids white noise clumping but is "not error diffusion" quality (https://bartwronski.com/2016/10/30/dithering-part-three-real-world-2d-quantization-dithering/). 64x64 fits any C64 bitmap by tiling, and a 4096-entry table is trivial offline. **[inference]**
- Bayer vs blue noise: Wronski says Bayer has strong spectral spikes causing "very visible unpleasant patterns" and advises avoiding it; most engines used up to 4x4 (same page). Surma notes Bayer brightens near-black and Obra Dinn uses blue noise for environments and Bayer for people/objects (https://surma.dev/things/ditherpunk/).
- Interleaved gradient noise (Jimenez): ALU-only, little low-frequency content, but residual hatching and aliasing (Wronski, above). R2 (rank-1 lattice fract(x/g + y/g^2), g about 1.3247) is a low-discrepancy grid, and Wolfe's post does not claim either R2 or IGN approximates blue noise (https://blog.demofox.org/2022/02/01/two-low-discrepancy-grids-plus-shaped-sampling-ldg-and-r2-ldg/). Marginal for a converter that can afford a baked blue-noise tile.
- Why blue noise for small images: pixel-independent (cheap, tileable, animation-safe) while avoiding Bayer's crosshatch; Wronski also notes blue noise holds up better when filtered or used temporally (hinted, deferred to later posts in that series). The "small images" preference itself is **[inference]**.

## 4. Structure-aware, perceptual, gamma-correct

- Structure-aware halftoning (Pang, Qu, Wong, Cohen-Or, Heng, SIGGRAPH 2008): optimisation of an objective with a structure term and a tone term; blue-noise-like output; no edge detector; best in their user study. The project page does not name SSIM explicitly (https://ttwong12.github.io/papers/structurehalftone/structurehalftone.html ; https://history.siggraph.org/?p=112894). Binary-only formulation; extension to 16-colour palettes with per-cell limits is not in the cited material. Iterative, so expensive: it is a candidate for an offline "high quality" mode. **[inference]**
- Pappas-Neuhoff model-based halftoning: references found only as search listings (SPIE "Blue noise and model-based halftoning" https://ebooks.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/2179/0000/Blue-noise-and-model-based-halftoning/10.1117/12.172669.full ; IS&T paper https://www.imaging.org/common/uploaded%20files/pdfs/Papers/1999/RP-0-93/1804.pdf). Content not fetched, so mechanism is **[unverified]** here. General idea (minimise error after a display/eye blur model) is the same principle as comparing blurred output to blurred source, which is simple to implement as a scoring step. **[inference]**
- Gamma: dithering in sRGB makes gradients "look too bright too quickly"; convert to linear first (https://surma.dev/things/ditherpunk/). Wikipedia FS page also says linearise. Yliluoma uses gamma 2.2 in algorithms 2/3 and discusses gamma-aware mixing a^g, mix, ^(1/g) (https://bisqwit.iki.fi/story/howto/dither/jy/). For C64 the mixing happens on a CRT/LCD in the viewer's eye, so mix in linear light using the actual C64 palette values. **[inference]**
- Perceptual spaces: Oklab improves perceptual uniformity and lightness prediction versus CIELAB and fixes hue/lightness shifts in blues; distance is Euclidean (L,a,b). The same page cautions that Oklab is not suited to blending/processing, where linear RGB is appropriate (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklab_color_space). Practical reading: pick nearest/best colours by Oklab (or luma-weighted) distance, but compute the averaged mix in linear RGB, then convert for comparison. **[inference]**. Yliluoma notes CIE76 produced scattered yellow pixels and CIE-based comparison "works better for some pictures than others" (his page), a caution that fancy metrics are not a free win.

## 5. Pixel-art community practice

- Aseprite: colour-mode conversion offers ordered (default Bayer 8x8, selectable matrices), "old" (undocumented difference), error-diffusion (Floyd-Steinberg, amount adjustable), none. Source: https://community.aseprite.org/t/what-are-the-changepixelformat-dithering-modes/8385 (user thread, not official docs; official docs page was unreachable).
- Pixel Parmesan: checkerboard is the most common simple blend; any pattern can be inverted; for gradients vary rows/columns between steps; for high contrast add intermediate colours rather than many patterns between two; dithering "rarely necessary in modern work"; avoid on tiny or animated sprites; noise dither is "a bit sloppy"; examples of hand dithering (Eternal Champions, Lionheart) and of tools (Deluxe Paint gradient fill, masks with patterns, HD Index Painting). Source: https://pixelparmesan.com/dithering-for-pixel-artists
- Divoom guide: patterns = checkerboard 1:1, ordered Bayer (cleaner multi-step but crosshatch at small sizes), random; use for limited palettes (8/16/32), gradients; avoid in areas narrower than about 8-10 px, when the palette already has the needed mid-tone, or when it fails a zoom-out test; limit to one or two areas. Source: https://divoom.com/blogs/setup-ideas/pixel-art-dithering-when-to-use-and-stop (commercial blog, low authority).
- Lospec: the tutorial index lists dithering tutorials but gives no explicit usage guidance on that page (https://lospec.com/pixel-art-tutorials/tags/dithering). I could not verify specific Lospec claims beyond that.
- Hand + auto mixing: community practice is to auto-dither smooth gradients and hand-fix edges and small features. The Pixel Parmesan examples above support hand dithering as the norm historically; the "mix both" workflow is **[inference]** supported by mask/brush tools described there. For a converter: offer per-region dither strength/masks, and an edge-aware suppression.
- Banding vs dithering: neither page treats banding as a standalone topic. Divoom describes undithered gradients as "hard colour bands". The C64 consequence is that with 4 colours per cell, banding is often preferable in detailed areas and dithering in smooth ones. **[inference]**

## 6. Temporal / interlace (flicker) colour mixing

- C64 modes: MCI alternates two multicolour pictures per frame, one shifted a pixel sideways, giving finer image and larger palette; FLI gives each 4x8 cell the full palette via "bad line" trick; IFLI combines MCI and FLI (https://www.studiostyle.sk/dmagic/gallery/gfxmodes.htm). The page does not discuss luminance or call it "colour mixing".
- Flicker visibility evidence (Atari 2600 thread, not C64): flicker is "annoying if the colors being mixed are too different" (SeaGtGruff), luminance differences are the key cause, rates of 20 Hz (NTSC/60) or 17 Hz (PAL/50) for a 3-frame cycle are more noticeable than 30/25 Hz for a 2-frame cycle, mixes can look "muddy", and real displays/emulator phosphor blend differ. Source: https://forums.atariage.com/topic/98820-mixing-colors-with-flicker/ . Note: the 2-frame figures are as stated there; PAL C64 2-frame alternation is 25 Hz.
- Stability rules that follow (**[inference]** from the above plus Yliluoma's "pixel depends only on itself"): (1) only alternate colour pairs of similar luminance (use a luma-difference threshold, like Yliluoma algorithm 3's luminance-spread filter); (2) keep the two-frame structure static, i.e. no error diffusion across frames and no per-frame noise, so use a fixed blue-noise/Bayer phase per frame (Riemersma's locality also helps, per https://www.compuphase.com/riemer.htm); (3) on a per-pixel basis choose the alternation (A/B) such that mean linear luminance is correct, rather than rendering two independent dithers; (4) flickering mixes work best in flat, low-detail areas, avoid near edges.
- Amiga specifics: no fetched source on Amiga interlace dithering; omitted.

## 7. Ranked recommendation for a 16-colour, 4-colours-per-cell C64 converter

Because of the per-cell colour limit, the real problem is joint (cell palette selection) + (dither within cell). Algorithms below assume the converter first picks the cell's 4 colours, then dithers with the restricted set, or iterates between the two. **[inference]** Ranking is judgment, supported by the cited properties.

Implement first:
1. **Linear-light, palette-restricted ordered dithering with blue-noise tile (64x64, torus-baked) and a Bayer 4x4/8x8 option.** Cheap, deterministic, position-only (animation/flicker-safe, cell-local, parallelisable, no error leaking across cell boundaries). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dither , https://surma.dev/things/ditherpunk/ , https://bartwronski.com/2016/10/30/dithering-part-three-real-world-2d-quantization-dithering/ .
2. **Yliluoma-style candidate-list mixing (algorithm 2 first, algorithm 3 style as "quality" mode) over the 4 colours available in the cell,** gamma-aware, luminance-sorted, with a luma-spread cap. Source: https://bisqwit.iki.fi/story/howto/dither/jy/ . Candidate list size can be 2-4 given the cell constraint, so cost is negligible.
3. **Floyd-Steinberg with serpentine scan, linearised, with error attenuation/clamp, plus Atkinson as the "high contrast" preset.** The reference baseline users expect; Atkinson for low-tone-count crispness. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floyd%E2%80%93Steinberg_dithering , https://tannerhelland.com/2012/12/28/dithering-eleven-algorithms-source-code.html . Caveat: error crossing cell boundaries after the cell's palette is fixed can produce colour-clash artefacts. **[inference]**
4. **Per-region dither strength/mask and edge-aware suppression** (artists' practice: dither smooth areas, not small details). Sources: Pixel Parmesan and Divoom above.
5. **Luma-bounded temporal pair dithering for MCI/IFLI-style output**, only after the static pipeline works. Sources: AtariAge thread and studiostyle page above.

Next tier (worth adding once the above work):
6. Riemersma (Hilbert) as an alternative organic, local dither. Source: https://www.compuphase.com/riemer.htm
7. Jarvis/Stucki/Burkes/Sierra family as selectable kernels (cheap to add once a generic diffusion routine exists; differences are subtle per Helland).
8. A post-hoc iterative refinement that minimises blurred-output vs blurred-source error (model-based halftoning style; Pappas-Neuhoff concept, unverified here) or a structure+tone objective (Pang et al. 2008). Offline "max quality" mode.

Marginal / skip initially:
- Ostromoukhov, Zhou-Fang: tuned for grey-level 1-bit halftoning with per-level tables; no published colour/arbitrary-palette results in the cited abstracts. Gains for a 16-colour target are speculative.
- Shiau-Fan, Stevenson-Arce: patented/unverified, no clear benefit.
- IGN, R2: cheap procedural alternatives to a baked blue-noise tile; unnecessary offline.
- Algorithm 1 of Yliluoma with full-palette pair search: subsumed by 2/3 under a 4-colour cell limit.
- Perceptual-space choice: use Oklab (or luma-weighted RGB) for colour selection only; do not blend in Oklab (Wikipedia caution). Avoid CIE76. Treat CIEDE2000 as optional; Yliluoma found metric choice picture-dependent.
