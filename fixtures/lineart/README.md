# lineart — Line art (hi-res bitmap, 2 colours per 8×8 cell)

Black-ink tactical sketch map: contours, stream, dashed track, pine symbols, trench line, unit boxes, north arrow, scale bar.

## What it tests

The only hi-res fixture: pure two-colour output, thin strokes crossing cell boundaries, anti-aliased source edges that must threshold cleanly. Note: the generator added a letter “N” at the north arrow despite the prompt; it is left in as realistic input.

## Known: yellow in a few cells (#155)

`source.png` is pure greyscale, but the hi-res result, png2c64's baseline included, shows yellow in 12 cells (90 pixels), among them the hut's peak: cells (8, 3), (9, 9), (12, 10), (14, 11), (15, 11), (26, 18), (27, 3), (30, 6), (31, 9), (35, 12), (36, 14), (38, 20). Since #181 and #190 our output is png2c64's pixel for pixel, so this is png2c64's behaviour, ported as it is:

- For `none` and the diffusion methods, png2c64 takes each cell's colours from a Floyd–Steinberg dither of the whole picture against all 16 colours (`global_fs_indices`), most used first.
- Where a thin line crosses a near-white cell, that dither spreads the line's darkness across several colours. Yellow, the lightest colour after white, comes out more often than any dark one: in cell (8, 3) the histogram is 51 white, 5 yellow, 2 grey and at most 2 of any dark colour.
- So the cell's pair is white + yellow, and the line's pixels, being nearer yellow than white, are drawn yellow.

Not the pair bias or Force weighting (neither is in this path), and not a set-search error in the usual sense: the brute-force search would pick white + black here, but png2c64 replaces its choice with the dither's. Changing it would be a divergence from png2c64 (for example, ranking the histogram by lightness range rather than count), to be added as a named option and judged on the sheet, not a fix.
